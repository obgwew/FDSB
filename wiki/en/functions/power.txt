<dash>
name: $power
desc: Raises one number to the power of another.
category: Math & Numbers

<block type="text" title="How does the command work?">
text: $power takes a base and an exponent and works out the base raised to that power. You can drop it straight inside a line of text and the bot prints the answer in the channel, or keep it on a line of its own while you are working out the numbers. A whole-number answer arrives without a decimal tail, so raising 2 to the power of 10 shows up as plain 1024.
</block>

<block type="list" title="How to use it in the app">
item: Open the Commands tab
item: Tap + to start a new command
item: Give it a name in the Name field
item: Set the Prefix field to !calc
item: Write your script in the editor
item: Paste the line below wherever you need it
item: Tap Save
</block>

<block type="code" title="Syntax">
text: $power[base; exponent]
</block>

<block type="code" title="Ready-to-copy Example">
text: $sendMessage[$power[2; 10]]
</block>

<block type="callout" kind="note">
text: $power[2; 10] gives 1024, $power[3; 3] gives 27 and $power[5; 0] gives 1. Exactly two numbers are needed, and text in either spot stops the command with an error.
</block>

<block type="callout" kind="warning">
text: A negative base raised to a fractional exponent has no real answer, and a result too large to hold is refused as well. Both cases stop the command rather than printing a number you could not use.
</block>
</dash>

<details>
syntax: $power[base; exponent]
param: base|number|Required|The number being raised, such as 2, 10 or 3.5
param: exponent|number|Required|How many times to raise it, such as 3, 10 or 0.5
</details>
